What we are actually building

Three rooms, one loop

Room 1

Diagnostic Lab

Solve a physics puzzle — air–fuel ratio, spark timing, compression — and the part unlocks.

→
Room 2

Assembly Bay

Drag the part onto the blueprint. It only fits if the parts it depends on are already there.

→
Room 3

Dyno Chamber

Rev it. The engine sounds and revs differently depending on what you actually built.

The loop is the lesson. A child learns the four-stroke cycle because their engine will not start without a spark plug — not because a quiz asked them to name one.

The one thing that must feel good: the snap. If bolting a part on is not satisfying, no amount of STEM content saves the game.

01 · Ideation Gemini chat
Copy this — Stage 1

Research & brainstorm → PRD

You are a STEM game designer and a mechanical engineer. Be blunt.

Concept: "PistonCraft", a browser game for ages 8-16. Players solve physics
puzzles to earn engine parts, snap them together in correct mechanical order,
then rev the finished engine on a dyno.

Give me:

1. THREE CRITICAL FINDINGS — why earning and assembling parts teaches
   thermodynamics better than a quiz does. Cite the mechanism, not vibes.

2. THE 5-LINE DECISION RECORD — exactly five lines, no prose:
   CHOSEN CONCEPT / TARGET AUDIENCE / WHY THIS WINS /
   WILL FAIL IF / OUT OF SCOPE

3. THE SEVEN PARTS — for each: the real STEM principle it teaches
   in one sentence a 10-year-old understands, and what breaks in the
   engine if it is missing.

4. THE HONEST RISK — name the single thing most likely to make
   this boring, and what you would cut to prevent it.

Do not write an introduction. Start at heading 1.
01 · Ideation
The artifact

A decision you can be held to

The 5-line record

  • Concept: Diagnostic Lab → Assembly Bay → Dyno
  • Target: Ages 8–16, touch-first, plays on a school tablet
  • Why it wins: every part visibly mounts and audibly changes the engine
  • Will fail if: the snap feels mushy or the engine sounds fake
  • Out of scope: 3D CAD, multiplayer, gearboxes, accounts

The 7 parts and what they teach

  • Engine block — the frame that holds combustion
  • Crankshaft — up-and-down becomes round-and-round
  • Pistons & rods — squeeze a gas and it heats
  • Cylinder head & valves — timing lets air in and out
  • Spark plugs — a spark at the wrong moment wastes the stroke
  • Intake manifold — 14.7 : 1 air to fuel, or it coughs
  • Turbocharger — waste exhaust does useful work

03 · Code AI Studio
Measured, not assumed

Layers do not hold themselves

What actually happened. The polite Layer 1 prompt — shell, blueprint, inventory — came back with a Web Audio synthesiser, a four-stroke animation loop, a dyno curve and a P–V diagram. It built all four layers at once, badly, on the first message.

Why it happens

  • "Return complete code with zero placeholders" reads as build everything
  • Naming the Lab and Dyno tabs invites the model to fill them
  • Nothing in the prompt said the word not

The fix — a scope fence

  • Name the forbidden features explicitly, one by one
  • Say what the empty tabs render instead
  • Give a tripwire: "if you are writing an oscillator, stop"

Every layer prompt in this deck carries a scope fence. That is why they look defensive — they had to be.

03 · Code AI Studio
Before you paste Layer 2

Accept or reject — 60 seconds

Accept if

  • Tab key reaches every part card, Enter selects one
  • The focus ring is clearly visible on the dark background
  • Seven slots, dashed cyan, each labelled and readable at arm's length
  • The Lab and Dyno tabs are empty on purpose
  • Search the code for # — hex only appears in index.css

Reject and re-prompt if

  • Anything animates, plays a sound, or shows a gauge
  • Labels are 10 or 11px — a real and repeated failure
  • It generated a single HTML file with CDN script tags
  • Parts are emoji instead of drawn SVG
  • The tray scrolls off-screen at 390px wide

Re-prompt, do not patch by hand. Say which self-check failed and ask for that fix only — hand edits get overwritten by the next layer.

Layer 3: the dyno & the engine note

Add LAYER 3 ONLY to PistonCraft: the Dyno Chamber.

=== SCOPE FENCE ===
Do not touch the Assembly Bay or Lab code. No Gemini call yet.

=== PERFORMANCE CONTRACT - NOT OPTIONAL ===
RPM, crank angle and piston position live in useRef, mutated inside
one requestAnimationFrame loop. Never setState per frame. useState is
only for what a human changes: throttle, tab, on/off. Must hold 60fps
on a low-end tablet. Cancel the loop and close the audio on unmount.

=== 1. THE CHAMBER ===
START ENGINE, tachometer 0-8000 with redline at 6500, a throttle
slider that also takes arrow keys, and a live HP/torque curve.

=== 2. FOUR-STROKE CANVAS - cylinder 1 in cross-section ===
Intake: valve opens, piston down, mist in. Compression: valves shut,
piston up. Power: plug fires, piston driven down. Exhaust: gas out.
Name the stroke on screen in words - that label IS the lesson.

=== 3. THE SOUND ===
Web Audio only. Sawtooth through a lowpass, frequency follows RPM.
AudioContext created on the FIRST CLICK, never on load, resume()
awaited. No turbo, no spool; a missing plug misfires. Sound starts
off, and the toggle truly silences it.

Layer 4: Chief Otto & the polish pass

Add LAYER 4 ONLY to PistonCraft: the AI tutor and final polish.

=== SCOPE FENCE ===
No new game mechanics. No new screens. Do not rebalance the puzzles.

=== 1. CHIEF OTTO ===
A side panel, opened from the HUD, closed with Escape.
Quick chips: "Why do I need a turbo?", "Torque or horsepower?",
"Why did it misfire?" - each sends a question with the current engine
state attached, so the answer is about the child's engine, not engines.
System prompt: "You are Chief Otto, a race engineer teaching a curious
10-year-old. Three sentences maximum. Plain words. Never condescend."

=== 2. THE FALLBACK - THIS IS THE DEMO-SAFETY FEATURE ===
If the API key is missing, the call fails, or it takes over 5 seconds,
answer from a local dictionary of 10 written explanations instead.
The panel must never show a spinner that never resolves, and never a
raw error. Test it by deleting the key: the tutor still answers.
The API key is read server-side. Never inline it in client code.

=== 3. POLISH ===
Every touch target >= --touch-min. Full keyboard path from load to a
revving engine. Reset button. Check 390x844 and 1280x720 for clipping.
Then re-read DESIGN.md, strip any raw hex or arbitrary value that crept
in over layers 2-4, and list every replacement.
03 · Code AI Studio
When it goes wrong

Four repair prompts

It built too much

Scope creep

"You built features from a later layer. Remove the dyno, audio and animation entirely and restore the placeholder panel. Layer 1 is the shell only."

It looks generated

Tokens ignored

"Raw hex and arbitrary Tailwind values appear in components. Re-read DESIGN.md, move every colour into a token, and list each replacement."

Silent or crashing audio

AudioContext

"AudioContext must be created inside the first click handler and resume() awaited. It is being created on load, which browsers block."

It stutters

setState per frame

"RPM is in React state and re-renders every frame. Move RPM and crank angle into useRef, mutate inside the rAF loop, and keep useState for user actions only."

Always name the violated rule. "It looks bad" gets you a different bad. "DESIGN.md says no arbitrary values and line 40 has text-[10px]" gets you a fix.

Chaos script & rubric audit

You are a QA engineer and a hackathon judge. Here is my PistonCraft code.

PART A - CHAOS SCRIPT. For each, tell me pass or fail and the exact fix:
1. Click sound-on as the very first action. Does audio start with no
   console exception?
2. Place parts in deliberately wrong order five times. Does state stay
   clean, and is every message kind rather than scolding?
3. Hold the throttle at redline for 60 seconds. Any frame drop or
   growing memory?
4. Switch tabs mid-rev. Does the rAF loop cancel and the audio stop?
5. Play the whole game with the keyboard only, from load to a revving
   engine. Where exactly does it become impossible?
6. Delete the API key and ask Chief Otto a question. Does the fallback
   answer, with no spinner and no raw error?
7. Load at 390x844. What clips, overlaps or scrolls off?

PART B - SCORE IT OUT OF 100 and justify every deduction:
- STEM depth: does the child learn the mechanism, or just the words? (30)
- Game feel: is the snap satisfying, is the engine convincing? (30)
- Visual consistency: any raw hex, any font under 14px? (20)
- Gemini integration: does the tutor use the child's own engine state? (20)

End with the three fixes with the best score-per-hour, in order.
