00 · The reference
Should you attach the store screenshots?

Use the screenshots. Don't copy them.

Why attaching them helps

  • Gemini reads images well — palette, proportion and density come back far more accurate than from prose
  • You get real hex values, not my guess at "grass green"
  • Head-to-body ratio and outline weight are almost impossible to specify in words
  • It shows you what the HUD leaves empty, which is the hard part

Why "make it look like this" is the wrong ask

  • Store screenshots are the developer's copyrighted assets
  • "Clone this" produces a derivative, and judges recognise a reskin
  • Their character designs and logo are their IP, not genre convention
  • You end up with their game, slightly worse — which scores badly on originality

The method that gets you both: attach the images → ask for a measurable spec → close the images → build from the text. This is how designers have always used reference, and the written spec is what goes into AI Studio anyway.

00 · The reference
Decide this before you prompt anything

Cut the multiplayer. All of it.

Fifteen real players in a three-minute match needs authoritative servers, state sync, lag compensation, matchmaking and anti-cheat. That is a team and a budget, not a prompt. An AI model will happily generate something that looks like netcode and does not work, and you will not find out until demo day.

Not building

  • Real-time networking of any kind
  • Matchmaking, lobbies, parties
  • Server-authoritative state
  • Accounts, ranks, leaderboards
  • Chat between real humans

Building instead — reads identically

  • One human, 14 bots, same 15-fighter match
  • Same three-minute round, same shrinking zone
  • Same loot, bushes, damage numbers, hit feel
  • Quick-chat bubbles fired by bot AI
  • Runs offline, so the venue wifi cannot kill it

A judge watching a demo cannot tell. They see a crowded arena, a closing circle and a win screen. Bots buy you the entire pitch for a fraction of the risk — and they are the honest engineering answer, not a compromise.

interface Entity {
  kind: Kind;   alive: boolean;
  x: number;    y: number;
  vx: number;   vy: number;
  radius: number;

03 · Code AI Studio
The one contract that decides everything

React must not know the game is running

=== THE PERFORMANCE CONTRACT - REPEAT IN EVERY LAYER PROMPT ===
This is a real-time game, not a page. Get this wrong and nothing works.

- ALL moving state - positions, velocities, health, cooldowns, camera -
  lives in useRef objects and is mutated in place. Never in useState.
- ONE requestAnimationFrame loop, started in a useEffect with an empty
  dependency array and cancelled in its cleanup.
- NEVER call a setState function inside that loop. Not once.
  useState is only for what a human changes between frames: pause,
  hero select, the end-of-match screen.
- Fixed timestep: accumulate elapsed ms, step the simulation in fixed
  1/60s increments, clamp the accumulator to 250ms so a backgrounded
  tab cannot spiral. Identical behaviour on a 60Hz and a 144Hz screen.
- Render to ONE canvas. Entities are never DOM nodes.
- Size the canvas backing store to width * devicePixelRatio.

Why it must be repeated: layer 3 adds bots and layer 4 adds particles. Those are exactly the prompts where a model reaches for useState — and by then you have a stutter with no obvious cause.

03 · Code AI Studio
Before you paste Layer 2

Accept or reject — 60 seconds

Accept if

  • The character moves smoothly on WASD and on a dragged joystick
  • The camera follows without jitter and stops at the arena edge
  • React DevTools shows no re-render while you move
  • Search the code: useState appears zero times, or only for pause
  • Hex codes appear only in index.css — including the canvas palette

Reject and re-prompt if

  • Anything shoots, spawns or takes damage
  • Position is held in useState — this is the fatal one
  • There is no fixed timestep, just deltaTime straight from rAF
  • Entities are <div>s instead of canvas draws
  • The canvas is blurry on a phone — no devicePixelRatio

Position in useState is not a style problem. Every later layer builds on the loop's shape. Fix it now or rewrite everything at layer 3.

Layer 2: shooting, and how it feels

Add LAYER 2 ONLY to Tiny Royale. Re-read DESIGN.md and obey it.
Repeat the performance contract - it still applies to everything below.

=== SCOPE FENCE ===
Do NOT build bot AI, the shrinking zone, loot, pickups, heroes or
skills. Add ONE stationary practice dummy that respawns 2s after it
dies. That is the only new fighter. Do not restyle Layer 1.

=== 1. SHOOTING ===
Auto-fire at the nearest fighter in range and line of sight. No aim
control - this is a one-thumb game. Bullets come from a fixed pool of
256, reused, never allocated in the loop. Circle collision; on hit,
apply damage then despawn.

=== 2. THE FEEL - THIS IS THE POINT OF THE LAYER ===
- A damage number rises and fades from the hit point: --color-dmg-out
  when the player deals it, --color-dmg-in when the player takes it.
- An orange spark ring flashes on the hit fighter for ~120ms.
- Muzzle flash at the barrel, a short tracer behind each bullet.
- The victim flinches back a few units. Nothing else knockbacks.
- Damage numbers and sparks come from fixed pools too.

=== 3. HEALTH ===
Every fighter has a canvas HP bar above its head, plus name and level.
At 0 HP: a burst, the body fades over 400ms, then the slot is freed.
The player's HP drives the HUD bar - throttled, never per frame.
03 · Code AI Studio
Copy this — Layer 3

Layer 3: fourteen bots and a closing circle

Add LAYER 3 ONLY to Tiny Royale: the match. Repeat the performance
contract. After this layer the game is complete and playable.

=== SCOPE FENCE ===
Do NOT build heroes, skills, loot, pickups, bush concealment or
quick chat. Every fighter has the same stats and the same gun.

=== 1. FOURTEEN BOTS - A FOUR-STATE MACHINE, NOTHING CLEVERER ===
WANDER: walk to a random point, repath on arrival.
SEEK: nearest fighter within 400 units - close to firing range.
FIGHT: in range, strafe sideways and let auto-fire work.
FLEE: below 30% HP, run from the nearest threat for 3 seconds.
Bots re-evaluate every 300ms, NOT every frame, staggered so all 14
never think on one frame. Each gets a 150-350ms reaction delay so they
are not inhumanly precise. Bots take zone damage and can die to it.

=== 2. THE ZONE ===
Closes from 0:45 in four steps, ending at ~15% of the arena by 2:40.
A --color-zone boundary with the outside dimmed. Outside costs 3 HP/s
at first, rising each step: briefly survivable, fatal to camp in.

=== 3. MATCH FLOW ===
A 3:00 countdown and a live "N ALIVE" counter in the DOM HUD, updated
a few times a second - never per frame. Ends when one remains or time
runs out (most kills wins), then a result screen with placement,
kills, damage and a PLAY AGAIN that resets every pool explicitly.

Layer 4: heroes, loot and juice

Add LAYER 4 ONLY to Tiny Royale: depth and polish. Repeat the

=== 1. THREE HEROES, PICKED BEFORE THE MATCH ===
Ranger: baseline. Skill DASH, 6s - 200 units instantly, brief i-frames.

=== 2. LOOT ===
14 spawn points. Weapons: SMG, shotgun, sniper - each visibly changes

=== 3. BUSHES BECOME REAL ===

Screenshake on kills and grenades - offset the camera a few units for

("I'm under siege!", "let me help you!") on real triggers, not random.

03 · Code AI Studio
When it goes wrong

Four repair prompts

It stutters

State in the loop

"The game re-renders every frame. Move all positions, velocities, health and cooldowns into useRef, mutate them in the rAF loop, and keep useState only for pause, hero select and the result screen."

It slows down over time

Allocation per frame

"Bullets and damage numbers are allocated with new or push each frame. Use fixed-size pools with an alive flag, reused. Nothing is allocated inside the loop."

Bots feel robotic

No reaction delay

"Bots aim instantly and all think on the same frame. Add a 150–350ms reaction delay, stagger their 300ms re-evaluation, and make them strafe while firing."

It looks flat

Missing juice

"Kills have no weight. Add ~150ms screenshake, a brief hit-stop, and a spark burst on death — using only DESIGN.md tokens and existing pools."

Name the violated rule, not your disappointment. "It feels laggy" gets you a different lag; "position is in useState, the contract says useRef" gets you a fix.

Frame budget & rubric audit

You are a game QA engineer and a hackathon judge. Here is my Tiny Royale code.

PART A - THE CHAOS SCRIPT. Pass or fail, plus the exact fix:
1. Profile the busiest moment - 15 fighters alive, bullets flying.
   Does any frame exceed 16ms, and what is on the stack?
2. Play 10 matches to completion. Does every one resolve inside 3:00,
   or can two survivors wander apart and stalemate?
3. Background the tab 30s and return. Does the accumulator clamp hold,
   or does the sim fast-forward?
4. Any new, push, map, filter or object literal inside update or
   render? List every one with file and line.
5. Kill the player at 0:10 and at 2:59. Is the placement right both?
6. PLAY AGAIN five times without reloading. Any state leaking - stale
   bullets, wrong count, growing memory?
7. Landscape phone, 844x390. Is the joystick under a thumb and the
   skill button reachable without shifting grip?
8. Any raw hex outside index.css, including canvas draw code?

PART B - SCORE IT OUT OF 100 and justify every deduction:
- Game feel: does a kill land with weight? (30)
- Bots: do they read as opponents or as targets? (25)
- Performance: does it hold 60fps at peak? (25)
- Visual consistency: tokens honoured, no font under 14px? (20)

End with the three fixes with the best score-per-hour, in order.
