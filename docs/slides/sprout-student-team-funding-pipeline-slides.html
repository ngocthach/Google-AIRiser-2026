Sprout: find your team, grow the idea, raise the seed

00 · The problem
Why this exists

The idea was never the bottleneck

The student

"I can build it, alone."

A capable second-year with time and skills, who does not know the three people on the next floor who need exactly those skills.

The idea

"We needed one designer."

A project that dies at week three, not for lack of ambition, but because nobody could find one person who could draw.

The money

"It needed four million dong."

A prototype that never got built because there is no path between a good campus idea and a small amount of money.

All three are matching problems. Skills to projects, projects to people, projects to small amounts of money. None of them needs a new idea — they need a place where the three can see each other.

The measurable claim: teams formed per month, and the share of those teams that ship something. Not sign-ups. Sign-ups are how this kind of product lies to itself.

00 · Scope
Decide this before you prompt anything

Do not move real money. Not in the MVP.

Accepting payments needs a registered legal entity, a merchant account, KYC and AML checks, a refund policy and tax handling — and raising money from the public is a regulated activity. A student project holding other people's money is a liability you cannot code your way out of, and no AI model will warn you before generating a checkout page.

Not building

  • Card checkout, wallets, any live payment
  • Holding, escrowing or disbursing funds
  • Refunds, chargebacks, payouts
  • Anything that implies Sprout is a custodian

Building instead — the pledge

  • A backer commits an amount and a message
  • The bar fills, the backer is listed, the owner is notified
  • Settlement happens off-platform, between two humans
  • Every screen says pledged, never paid

The demo is identical — a goal, a filling bar, a list of backers. And the pledge-to-collected gap becomes your most honest metric, which is a better answer to a judge than a fake Stripe key.

00 · Scope
The failure nobody plans for

An empty marketplace is not a demo

What normally happens

You open the app in front of a judge. There are zero projects, because there are zero users, because there are zero projects. The judge sees an empty state and a very good login screen.

This is the single most common way a marketplace demo dies, and it has nothing to do with code quality.

What to do instead

  • Twelve real projects, written by hand, in the seed file from Layer 1
  • Named after things a Vietnamese campus would actually build
  • Varied funding: some at zero, one fully funded, some not fundraising
  • At launch: start with one faculty, not a university

"Project Alpha" and "Lorem ipsum" cost you the room. Seed content is design work, not filler — budget six hours for it and write it like you mean it.

01 · Ideation
The artifact

A decision you can be held to

The 5-line record

  • Concept: a campus board where projects staff themselves
  • User: undergrads with a project, or with free evenings
  • Why it wins: applying to a named role, not "join us"
  • Will fail if: the board is empty, or owners never reply
  • Out of scope: real payments, chat, orgs, search, admin

The 8 roles a student project actually needs

  • Builds the thing · Designs the thing · Writes the words
  • Talks to users · Handles the data · Films and edits
  • Keeps it organised · Knows the subject

Why plain language: "Backend Engineer" excludes the second-year who could do it. "Builds the thing" does not.

02 · Blueprint
The artifact — the data model

Five collections, one cheap read

users/{uid}
  displayName  faculty  year  skills[]  bio  photoURL
  contactEmail   // private: readable only by the owner

projects/{projectId}
  title  pitch  description  category  faculty  status
  ownerUid  memberUids[]  openRoles[{ id, title, need, filled }]
  fundingGoal  pledgedTotal  backerCount  memberCount  createdAt
  // the four counts are denormalised, and NOBODY writes them from a client

projects/{projectId}/applications/{uid}
  roleId  message  status: pending|accepted|declined|withdrawn  createdAt
  // an applicant reads their own; only the owner may list them all

projects/{projectId}/pledges/{uid}
  amount  message  isPublic  createdAt
  // immutable once written - a pledge is a promise, not a draft

projects/{projectId}/updates/{updateId}
  body  createdAt        // owner writes, everyone reads

Why counts live on the project: the Discover board renders twelve cards. Reading a subcollection per card is twelve extra round trips and a visibly slow board — the thing a judge notices first.

03 · Code AI Studio
The mistake I actually made

"Responsive" is a wish, not a spec

My first version of this prompt said, in full: "a responsive grid of project cards", plus a self-check reading "layout holds at 390x844 and 1440x900." The model satisfied both — truthfully — and the screen was still unusable.

What came back

  • One card alone in a 700px box, the rest of the screen empty
  • A 150px hole inside the card between pitch and progress bar
  • All 16 categories as a full-height left sidebar, scrolling
  • Five header buttons at identical weight, including "Seed Data"
  • Content stretched edge to edge on a wide monitor

What the spec has to say instead

  • 1 / 2 / 3 columns at 640 and 1024 — and never a fourth
  • margin-top:auto + line-clamp-2, so cards cannot hollow out
  • Chip row, "All" + 6 + More — not a sidebar
  • One primary action; dev controls behind import.meta.env.DEV
  • 1200px max width; 6 cards visible at 1440 before scrolling

The pattern: tokens fix colour and type, because a token is a number. Layout has no token — so if you do not give it numbers, the model picks its own, and it picks badly. Every adjective in a prompt is a place the model gets to decide.

The part that makes it production

Five rules, and the test for each

The ruleTest it in the Rules PlaygroundExpect
Only the owner edits a projectSigned in as a member, update titleDENY
An applicant cannot list other applicantsSigned in as applicant B, list applicationsDENY
An applicant reads their own applicationSigned in as A, get applications/AALLOW

The rule that will be wrong: the private contact email. It must be readable by its owner and by the owner of a project they applied to — and by nobody else. That is the one worth writing a test for, because getting it wrong leaks every student's email to every visitor.

When it goes wrong

Four repair prompts

It opened the database

Widened rules

"You changed the security rules to allow read or write if true. Revert that. The read is failing because the query does not match the rule — fix the query and explain which rule it violated."

It looks generated

Aesthetic drift

"New screens use shadows, pill buttons and a colour outside DESIGN.md. Re-read DESIGN.md, apply its DON'T list to every screen added since Layer 1, and list each change."

It added a checkout

Payment creep

"You added a payment form. Sprout never processes payments. Remove the library and every card field, and restore the pledge as a recorded commitment."

Counts drift

Client-side writes

"backerCount is written from the client and is now wrong. Move every count update into the same transaction as the write it counts, and deny client writes to those fields in the rules."

Name the violated rule, not your disappointment. "The design is off" gets a different off; "DESIGN.md forbids shadows and ProjectCard has shadow-md" gets a fix.
